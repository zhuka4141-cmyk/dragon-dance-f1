A neighbourhood.
A living dragon.

Every Mid-Autumn Festival, the streets of Tai Hang become a meeting place for craft, incense and collective movement. The fire dragon is more than a spectacle: it is a tradition carried by the people who build it, guide it and pass it on.

1880

A story rooted in Tai Hang

The tradition traces its beginnings to 1880. Local accounts tell of villagers performing a fire dragon dance to ward off a plague. That story lives on in a Mid-Autumn custom, bringing neighbours and generations together.

  1. 1880The beginnings of Tai Hang's fire dragon tradition.
  2. 2011Included in the third national list of intangible cultural heritage.
  3. 2017Included in Hong Kong's first Representative List of Intangible Cultural Heritage.

What brings the dragon to life?

Look closer: the glow begins with patient hands, simple materials and people moving together.

The body · Built by hand

Rattan, rope and pearl grass are shaped into a new dragon each year, with bamboo supports for its bearers. The work begins before the procession: binding, shaping and balancing each part. For us, that care echoes the path from a design to a carefully made race car.

The light · Incense, not flame

Burning incense is inserted into the dragon, tracing its shape with countless points of light. Smoke and glowing tips create the fire dragon's distinctive presence. Our amber particles and flowing lines take their cue from this light, rather than a dragon breathing fire.

The rhythm · Many hands, one dragon

Dragon pearls lead the way as drums and gongs set the rhythm. Bearers coordinate the head, body and tail through Tai Hang's streets. That shared attention inspires how our designers, makers and team managers work towards the same start line.

From the streets.
To the start line.

We see a connection between a dragon carried by its community and a race car brought to life by a team. Craft becomes engineering. Rhythm becomes coordination. A shared tradition becomes an invitation to create something together.
